# Course Evolution: 39th to 40th

Use this reference only for explaining duplicated lessons or selecting between conflicting versions.

## Overall

- Treat the 39th course as the fuller explanation of definitions, data caveats, and live examples.
- Treat the 40th course as the more standardized decision framework.
- Use the current merged workflow in `methods.md`; do not operate two separate versions.

## Material changes

### Category environment

- 39th: defines top-100 estimates, category-node data, concentration metrics, active listing count, and false-new-product traps.
- 40th: formalizes category → niche → product and adds operating discipline around profit and ad spend as a share of total sales.

### Blue-ocean niche

- 39th coarse example: top-100 monthly sales about 5,000–15,000, average monthly sales 50–150, average reviews 100–200.
- 40th coarse example: top-100 monthly sales about 3,000–15,000, average monthly sales 30–150, average reviews 200–300.
- Prefer the 40th gross-margin floor of about 30%.
- Prefer the 40th marketplace ACoS warning levels: about 40% for the US and 35% for Europe.
- The 40th adds a keyword-first review-dependence check: among roughly ten core keywords, seek at least 20% organic first-page listings with no more than 50 reviews.

### Multi-platform

- 39th: gives fuller platform-data interpretation, fulfillment checks, reverse-image search, infringement checks, and selling-point preservation.
- 40th: emphasizes the structural contradiction that popular cross-platform products usually already exist on Amazon; therefore begin from a low-attention niche.

### ABA

- 39th: covers general ABA keyword analysis, growth lists, review and ad checks, and the first/second-wave versus third/fourth-wave lifecycle.
- 40th: turns new-word selection into a clearer SOP and adds quick filters, predictable-versus-unpredictable demand shocks, crowding-speed checks, and four response options.

### Keyword data

- 39th: provides broader metric definitions and examples; example filters are looser, such as traffic-value around 3 and price around 15.
- 40th: uses a tighter example, such as traffic-value at most 2, price at least 10, average reviews at most 100, and excluding obvious seasonal words.
- The 40th explicitly allows strong organic-flow evidence to offset a weak expected-ACoS result, but only when supported by several comparable new products.

### Software product filtering

- 39th: explains why the common recent-launch + original-FBM model is now crowded and develops contrarian low-volume, high-price, small-marketplace, and natural-flow models.
- 40th: standardizes broad versus vertical filtering, reinforces manual false-positive removal, and uses monitoring for immature candidates.

### Social media

- 39th: gives detailed tool navigation and many cases across TikTok, Instagram, and Pinterest.
- 40th: formalizes the early-signal versus confirmation tradeoff, values repeated independent viral videos over one isolated post, expands sources, and uses tiered small-test or monitor decisions.

### Competitor-store reverse

- Present only as a standalone lesson in the 40th.
- Use it as another discovery source, then apply the same common validation layer.
